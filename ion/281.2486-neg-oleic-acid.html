At m/z 281.2486 in negative ion mode, this is [M-H]- of Oleic acid (C18H34O2, monoisotopic 282.2559).

  • This m/z is recomputed here from the molecular formula and the adduct, electron mass included.
  • Sources disagree on how to classify it — it is also reported as Ubiquitous matrix components, Miscellaneous background ions.
  • Accurate mass alone is not diagnostic here: other known chemical structures — not only the ones in this table — sit within a few ppm of it. Use retention time, the isotope pattern or MS2 to choose.

Where it comes from

Eppendorf polypropylene microcentrifuge tubes (blank Folch lipid extraction); Polypropylene disposable pipette tips (Eppendorf/Fisher).

Other sources report the same ion from: Skin/sebum transfer, hand creams, oleamide precursor; Plasma, FBS in media, skin and plasticware background; Ubiquitous fatty acid from skin/matrix/plastics.

Chromatography: standardized RT 5.56 min (RP-C18, Splash Lipidomix-standardized); RT 6.3 min (RP-C18 21.5 min gradient).

Lipophilicity: XLogP 6.5, late — a rough reversed-phase retention proxy, not a prediction.

Compounds leached from tubes, plates, tips, filters, membranes and syringes, which is the class the literature most often traces a mystery peak back to. More labware leachables →

How to recognise it

  • 281.2486 in ESI-; extremely common handling contaminant; also as oleate dimer 563.5
  • Ubiquitous background fatty acid; its amide (oleamide) is a separate plastic slip-agent contaminant
  • MaConDa theoretical adduct mass 281.2486036
