At m/z 221.1900 in positive ion mode, this is [M+H]+ of Butylated hydroxytoluene (C15H24O, monoisotopic 220.1827).

  • This m/z is recomputed here from the molecular formula and the adduct, electron mass included.
  • Sources disagree on how to classify it — it is also reported as Labware leachables.
  • Accurate mass alone is not diagnostic here: other known chemical structures — not only the ones in this table — sit within a few ppm of it. Use retention time, the isotope pattern or MS2 to choose.

Where it comes from

Antioxidant stabiliser in solvents (THF/ether) and plastics.

Other sources report the same ion from: Plastic tubing and caps, solvent stabiliser, food packaging; Nonylphenol ethoxylate degradation, plastic and rubber additives; Stabiliser in THF/ethers and antioxidant leached from plastics.

Chromatography: elutes late/lipophilic.

Lipophilicity: XLogP 5.3, late — a rough reversed-phase retention proxy, not a prediction.

Hindered-phenol and phosphite antioxidants (Irganox, Irgafos) and UV stabilisers that migrate out of plastic containers and tubing. More polymer antioxidants and stabilisers →

How to recognise it

  • Poor ESI+ response; often seen instead as the radical cation or as m/z 205 after methyl loss
  • Poor ESI+ response; negative mode strongly preferred
  • Ether/THF stabiliser; also leaches from tubing and filters; poor ESI+ response so often seen as radical cation or in APCI
