At m/z 297.0824 in positive ion mode, this is [M+H]+ of Octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane (C8H24O4Si4, monoisotopic 296.0752).

  • This m/z is recomputed here from the molecular formula and the adduct, electron mass included.
  • Sources disagree on how to classify it — it is also reported as Column and stationary-phase bleed.

Where it comes from

Ambient laboratory air, silicone septa and seals, personal care products, GC and LC column bleed.

Other sources report the same ion from: Siloxane background from endcapped silica columns, plus silicone seals and lubricants in the flow path; Airborne silicone, silicone rubber, septa, O-rings, greases; Silicone tubing, septa, O-rings, vial caps, glassware coatings, skin/hand cream.

Chromatography: ubiquitous across gradient; also present in blank and no-injection runs.

Cyclic (D4-D7) and linear dimethylsiloxanes spaced 74.0188 apart, from vial septa, tubing, seals, column bleed and ambient laboratory air. Their silicon isotope envelope (M+2 about 10-25%) is the giveaway. More siloxanes and silicones →

How to recognise it

  • Dimethylsiloxane repeat spacing 74.0188 between consecutive oligomers; airborne and septum-derived siloxane background; reduce with clean air supply…
  • Cyclic dimethylsiloxane; ubiquitous instrument background but genuinely elevated by endcapping residue on new silica columns. Series spacing 74.0188.…
  • First member of the airborne cyclic siloxane series listed by Stanford as 297, 371, 445, 519 (+74n); nominal m/z as printed
