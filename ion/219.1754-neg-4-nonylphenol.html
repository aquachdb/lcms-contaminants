Where it comes from

Hydrolysis or degradation product of NP-40 and Igepal detergents, plastics and environmental carryover.

Other sources report the same ion from: Plastic tubing and caps, solvent stabiliser (THF/ether), food packaging, gloves; Degradation product of nonylphenol ethoxylate surfactants, plastic and rubber additives; Antioxidant in plastic labware, filter membranes, THF and ether stabilizer.

Chromatography: elutes late/lipophilic.

Lipophilicity: XLogP 5.9, late — a rough reversed-phase retention proxy, not a prediction.

Ethoxylated surfactants -- Triton, Tween/polysorbate, alcohol and alkylphenol ethoxylates -- which ionise extremely well and appear as broad ethylene-oxide envelopes long after the glassware was washed. More non-ionic surfactants →

How to recognise it

  • Best detected in ESI-/APCI-; extremely common laboratory background from solvents and plastics
  • Technical material is a mixture of branched isomers giving a cluster of partially resolved peaks; isomeric with BHT
  • Branched isomer mixture giving a broad or multi-peak profile; parent phenol of the NP-40 ethoxylate series (ethoxamers covered elsewhere)
