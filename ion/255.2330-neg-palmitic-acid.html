Where it comes from

Eppendorf polypropylene microcentrifuge tubes (blank Folch lipid extraction); Polypropylene disposable pipette tips (Eppendorf/Fisher).

Other sources report the same ion from: Skin/sebum transfer, soaps, glove and paper residues; Plasma, FBS in media, and ubiquitous laboratory/skin background; Amide-embedded C16 reversed-phase columns (Supelco Discovery RP-Amide C16, Ascentis RP-Amide) after amide….

Chromatography: standardized RT 5.27 min (RP-C18, Splash Lipidomix-standardized); RT 6.0 min (RP-C18 21.5 min gradient).

Lipophilicity: XLogP 6.4, late — a rough reversed-phase retention proxy, not a prediction.

Compounds leached from tubes, plates, tips, filters, membranes and syringes, which is the class the literature most often traces a mystery peak back to. More labware leachables →

How to recognise it

  • Most abundant FFA background ion in ESI-; 255.2330 seen in nearly every blank; also as [M+Na]+ in ESI+
  • One of the most persistent negative-mode background ions in any LC-MS lab, present even in solvent blanks
  • Acyl half of a hydrolysed amide-embedded ligand. Palmitic acid is one of the most ubiquitous laboratory backgrounds, so only a rise that tracks…
