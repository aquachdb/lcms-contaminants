Where it comes from

Slip and mold-release additive in plastic tubes, tips and plates.

Other sources report the same ion from: Amide-embedded C16 reversed-phase columns; Also a common slip-agent background; Polymer lubricant/slip additive leaching from 5 mL plastic syringes.

Chromatography: elutes late/lipophilic.

Lipophilicity: XLogP 5.7, late — a rough reversed-phase retention proxy, not a prediction.

Oleamide, erucamide and stearamide, added to plastics as slip agents; notorious for appearing in positive mode from pipette tips and bags. More slip agents and fatty amides →

How to recognize it

  • Common companion of oleamide and stearamide from plasticware
  • Amide-embedded ligand fragment after C-N cleavage on the tether side. Heavily confounded with polyolefin slip agents, which give the identical ion
  • Tentatively identified alongside oleamide in leachate from plastic syringes; primary fatty acid amide, loses NH3 (-17) and H2O (-18) in MS2
